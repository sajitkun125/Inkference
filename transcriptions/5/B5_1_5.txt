Journal of the Voyage resumed.
April y.e 22.d 1774   After we had UNKNOWN(mooved) the Ship, we
soon were surrounded by a great number of boats, who came
out of mere curiosity to see the Ship: They soon found
we were their old friends, & a little while after, they brought
us fish, breadfruit, Apples & Cocoanuts for trade & got small
Nails in return. They had between the two Canoes a kind of trough
fixed both Ends secured with a kind of basket for the free entran-
ce of the Water, & in it great Number of small alive
Mullets, which fried made a most excellent dish. We got like-
wise some fine Bonitos.
april y.e 23.d   after dinner I went ashore, & looked about, & found
the Country in every respect improved. There were a good
many hogs, many new houses were erected & soft num-
bers of new Canoes were building & already built, chiefly
owing to the facility they got. to build them by the
Assistance of our tools of Iron. The Season we came in
was rainy, the rivers full of water, the country
even on the most distant hills covered with an a-
greeable verdure, & all plants grew in great luxu-
riancy: but we found no new ones, though we went pretty
high up on a hill. My Son was now sick of the dry gripes
as the Capt. M.r Whitehouse, & my Servt. & our cook
had been.
apr. y.e 24.th   I went the next morning early on shore &
crept over UNKNOWN(the Brookhill), & went to Opara: I found
a friendly Native, who went with me along.
I saw Tootahaiv's Mother & Apai, Too's Father,
& gave them presents. I went then in quest of plants
but found none new. I shot 2 ducks, & walked along
