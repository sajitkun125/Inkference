2/. South-Sea Otahaitee Matavai. April y.e 24.th 1774.
10 miles in the Country, where my new friends Opous 
house was: He immediately roasted me breadfruit, & ma-
de me a fine pudding, gave me Cocoanuts to drink
& presented me with a fine Ahou, & I gave him a
knife, a large Spike & beads: & to all his relations
some small Nails, & then returned back & saw in
my return near the Chiefs house, the two goats
Capt. Furneaux gave to Otu: the She-goat had
soon after kidded, & the 2 Kids were now nearly
as big as the mother & both She's so that if
the Natives take care the breed will soon be
spread over all the Island, & become a valuable
Acquisition especially if they brought them on the
hills, which are the true place for Goats. I saw
a new Figtree, & took specimens with me, & obser-
ved likewise a variety of the breadfruit Tree with nar-
row dissected leaves, which has an oblong prickly fruit.
The Tree which Burman calls Bromelia sylvestris
we observed to be a Dioecia, & though the Male flowers
were already withered & dry we saw however the fruit
was nearly related to the Palm-kind, & must there-
fore by no means be reckoned among the Brome-
lias. I came at Moonlight aboard & was much
fatigued & after supper soon went to bed
Next morning a great many Chiefs came on
board & some brought very large hogs, & got
hatchets & besides presents.
