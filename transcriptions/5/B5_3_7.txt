South Sea Otahaitee Matavai apr. y.e 24.th 1774   3.
The day I had been away ashore 43 fine Bonitos were bought
& served among all the Ship's Company, & afterwards
Greens & Breadfruit were plentifully served out.
April y.e 25.th   We had rain that day, & about noon Otu, his Sister
& brother came on board & brought likewise some hogs
& got hatchets & red feathers in return, which they admired
& coveted amazingly. The day after we had continual showers
of rain & a good deal of lightning & Thunder. Several Chiefs
came again on board & brought a good many hogs, for which
they got hatchets & other presents.
April y.e 26.th   About Evening it left of raining, & we had a fine
moonshine night. If the weather proves fair we intend next
morning to go to Opara, to see the Chiefs there. It rained
during night & in the morning several times. Several
flanel Blankets & other dressing-apparel were stolen from
a Man who was washing these things for Capt. Cook. We had in
the Morning several Chiefs on board. We went about 10 o'clock
on shore to Opara in order to see Otu, but we were very
much surprised seeing there a great fleet of their
War-Canoes on shore, all dressed & manned & quite ready
for action. The Stages where on the fighting men stand, were
mounted, all the Canoes had headpieces of carved-work
with a wooden figure of a man on top, several of which
were covered with black feathers. Some Sternpieces had
a piece of cloth spread over them. Every where bunches of
spears were sticking in the Canoes, & great many Stones
lay heaped up. The Battle-Axes were likewise disposed here
& there. The Warriors on the Stages, had long pieces of white
cloth hanging loose over their shoulders, other red ones
smaller over it & a brown one on top. The breast covered
with their breastplates, the head wrapt in a piece of cloth
